I have a graveyard of abandoned note-taking systems.

Notion databases I forgot to open. Obsidian vaults that felt like filing cabinets. Roam graphs so tangled they became unusable. Apple Notes with titles like "important stuff" from 2023.

After years of experimentation, I finally built something that works — not because it's the most sophisticated system, but because it's the most *honest* one.

## Why Most Systems Fail

The problem with most PKM (Personal Knowledge Management) systems isn't the software. It's the mental model.

We're trained to think of notes as a filing system — a place to store information for later retrieval. But that model leads to **collection without connection**. You end up with thousands of notes you never revisit, because retrieval requires remembering that something exists before you can find it.

The better model: **notes as thinking**.

## The Three Zones

My system has exactly three zones. No more.

### Zone 1: Capture (Daily Notes)
Every day gets a single note. Everything goes in here: ideas, todos, links, half-formed thoughts, meeting notes, shower thoughts. No organisation required. This is my inbox.

The key rule: **don't curate during capture**. Just get it down.

### Zone 2: Develop (Evergreen Notes)
When something from a daily note feels genuinely interesting or useful, I develop it into an "evergreen note" — a note designed to grow and link to other notes over time.

Evergreen notes follow three rules:
- **Atomic**: One idea per note
- **Titled as a claim**: "Learning in public accelerates growth" not "Learning in public"  
- **Linked**: Every note connects to at least two others

### Zone 3: Create (Projects)
When a cluster of evergreen notes converges into something, I start a project: a blog post, a talk, a library, a decision. Projects pull from evergreen notes, not from raw capture.

This is why I can write a blog post in a few hours — the thinking was already done.

## The Weekly Review

Every Sunday, I spend 20 minutes on two things:

1. **Process Zone 1** — move anything worth developing to Zone 2
2. **Connect Zone 2** — look for links between notes I've been developing separately

The connections are where the magic happens. Two ideas that seemed unrelated suddenly have something to say to each other.

## Tools Don't Matter (Much)

I use Obsidian, but I've seen this system work in Notion, Roam, Logseq, and plain text files. The tool matters less than the discipline.

What actually matters:
- **Daily consistency** — capture every day
- **Low friction** — the system must be faster than not using it
- **Regular processing** — captured notes that never get developed are just clutter

## Starting Small

If you're starting fresh, start with just Zone 1. Keep a daily note for 30 days. Don't worry about organisation. Just capture.

At the end of 30 days, look back. What kept coming up? What feels worth developing? Those are your first evergreen notes.

The system will grow around the ideas that matter to you — not the ideas that you *think* should matter to you.

That's the real insight after years of failed systems: the best PKM is the one that reflects how you actually think, not how you think you should think.
